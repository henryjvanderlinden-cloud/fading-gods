# Ideas

Dump zone. No obligation, no ordering, no shame. Nothing here is committed to and
nothing here has been measured. When an idea gets concrete enough to test, move it
to `open-points.md` as a question; when it's tested and in, it goes to
`design/rules.md`; when it's tested and out, `rejected.md` with the numbers.

Rough grouping only.

## Carried over, designed but never built

**Wandering hosts** — a third leg for the rock-paper-scissors. People who hold no
ground and move through it. Beat cities (raiders cannot be besieged), lose to
blessed ground (it stops movement), and are beaten by nothing else. Would score by
holding large sweeps of open country cheaply and losing it instantly. Absorbs the
prophet/hermit idea entirely — a host *is* a band carrying your presence.

**Prophets and manifestation** — see pressure where you have ears, route someone
from quiet country across loud country, prayer resumes, you manifest and intervene
directly, and the intervention becomes an epic carved on a wall. Ten to fifteen
turns end to end. Set-piece you earn rather than an event that fires.

**Martyrdom** — a prophet walking into a hostile city mostly fails, but a killed
prophet is remembered permanently. You would sometimes send someone knowing exactly
what happens, because you need the epic more than the city.

**Fission as the central choice** — when a settlement splits, do you go with the
splinter into quiet country and be a god again, or stay with the parent and become
its administration? Recurring every few decades, never the same twice.

**Temple as life support** — thick walls to stop the noise, ritual cleanliness as
noise reduction, an inner room. Every strange purity code in the ancient world read
as engineering. Temples as relay stations: you are present wherever you are still
housed and still kept.

**The standing stones at turn one belong to a previous cycle's failed player** —
generated from a synthetic prior campaign. Costs nothing; the most beautiful object
in the game becomes a warning.

## Cult, memory, distortion

**Writing distorts you** — the priests write down what they *believe* you said, at
the moment they write it, and how wrong it is depends on how audible you were that
year. Get deaf first and they fix an invention in stone forever. Remembered
accurately by nobody, or inaccurately by everyone. (Was implemented and working in
`game/previous/the-founding.html`.)

**Priesthood capture** — the instrument that extends your reach is the thing that
eventually replaces you. Careerists, status, a bureaucracy of belief that runs
perfectly without anyone hearing anything. Legible warning: the rites grow more
elaborate as the belief grows thinner.

**Syncretism as the real war** — a stronger cult does not destroy you, it absorbs
you. Consort, son, adversary, a saint's day. You survive, renamed, your epics
rewritten with someone else's name in the first line.

**Schism** — two of your own settlements at uneven strength, one attacks the other,
and it ends in two liturgies both claiming you and both partly wrong, with you
unable to arbitrate because you can't be heard in either.

**Emergent doctrine** — around year 20 and again at 35, the game names what your
people have become known for, derived from where your attention actually went
rather than what you intended. Named by outsiders. Possibly unfair.

## Mechanics not yet placed

**Seed control and map replay** — same map, different strategy. Would make A/B
testing honest.

**Rivers as a distinct feature** — passable but costly, and a natural place for
settlements, which would give the map more structure than blob islands.

**Sacred groves that cannot be reckoned** — a small number of tiles that resist
farmland permanently, worth fighting over.

**Stones that can be thrown down** — the rival spending a work to destroy one of
yours, rather than merely starving it.

**A wonder that restores a lost wonder**, once, at enormous cost. The bargain of
the late game.

**Seasons or bad years** — a lean year that halves growth everywhere. Would create
natural pauses in the exponential.

**Naming** — letting the player name settlements and stones, which is cheap and
does an enormous amount for attachment in a game about being remembered.

## Interface and presentation

**The interface becomes administrative over forty years** — direct manipulation
decaying into policy. Partly implemented; could go much further.

**No true map** — only maps someone drew, reflecting their skill and honesty.

**An end-of-game chronicle** written by a later historian, describing what became
of the valley, with your name in it or not.

**Show the year's events as a small illuminated marginalia** rather than a log
list.
