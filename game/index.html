You can only be remembered by the thing that deafens you.

You are an old power in a small valley, and you are fading. Nothing takes your power away: the people build enough instruments that they stop needing to ask, and then stop being able to hear. You put them here yourself, knowing what grows from them. Where you have walked the ground is blessed; where a settlement has counted and ploughed it the ground is reckoned, and you cannot set foot on it again — your own farmland walls you out exactly as theirs does. Every settlement that grows loud enough costs you the greatest wonder you have left, and opens a work of the settled in its place.

Each year you have three moves, one act — bless the ground you stand on, raise a stone, found a settlement, split one — and one intervention, which is a wonder while you still have wonders and a work once you do not. Forty years, and whoever holds the most ground at the end of them wins. You will end holding everything you can build and nothing you can call.
