Four rows, in the order the game happens in

Rick's layout, and the ordering is the argument. One act row plus one flowing
chip row becomes four labelled rows: action, wonders, teach, works. Read down,
that is the game — here is what you may do, here is what you still have, here is
the one thing that trades the one for the other, and here is what it bought. The
act row gains a label so it is one of the four rather than a different kind of
object sitting above them.

Teaching now sits physically between the two ladders it moves in opposite
directions. The single flowing row could not show that, because it put all three
groups on one line in whatever order they happened to wrap.

Knowingly given up: the old row turned from blessing-green to farmland-ochre
along one continuous horizontal axis over forty years, which was the best thing
about it. The gradient now runs down the rows instead of across them. Still
there, still narrated by nothing, and each row still empties from the left and
fills from the right. One axis rotated, not an axis lost.

It costs no screen, which was the thing to check, and it was measured in a real
browser at six sizes rather than asserted:

                        board   against the fold
  iPad 11   1194x745    880     102px below (was 108)
  iPad Air  1180x820    880     27px below
  laptop    1440x900    994     12px below (was 18)
  desktop   1920x1080   1272    all above the fold

Six pixels shorter than the two-row version. The fixed right-aligned label column
is tighter than the old flowing gap, and four short rows wrap less than two long
ones. No board size changed anywhere.

A latent layout bug this surfaced, which had been there all along. .play carries
margin:0 auto and is a grid item, and auto inline margins make a grid item
shrink-to-fit rather than stretch — so the playing column has always sized itself
to its widest child's max-content, and the max-width cap that OP-23's entire
tablet argument rests on was only ever reached by accident, because the old chip
row happened to be wider than the cap. Restacked into four rows the widest child
became the six wonder chips at 803px and the board silently dropped from 994 to
803 on a laptop: a 19% smaller board caused by a layout change two hundred pixels
away, in a rule that appears to have nothing to do with it. width:100% is what
the cap was always written to assume.

Worth having as a shape rather than a fix: the board's width was load-bearing on
the width of the chip row. Nobody would have gone looking for that. It was found
only because the board gets measured in a browser after every layout change, and
that habit is the whole of what OP-23 bought.

The tally moved out of #civic, where it had just been parked — render() rewrites
that element's innerHTML wholesale, so anything inside it is destroyed on the
first frame. It now sits beside the works rather than on a fifth row.

strength and past 800 came off the status line. Neither gates anything since
1.18, the works read taughtCount now, and a number on screen that no rule
consults is worse than absent because it reads as a thing to aim at. Dropping
them is also what lets the tally fit beside the works.
