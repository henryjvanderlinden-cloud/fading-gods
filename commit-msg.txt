A-32: what the settled side is for

Game 7, the first played from the agricultural side, and the first game
of any kind to end where the README says a game ends: six taught, no
working stone, every wonder struck through, ten percent of a body left
and no blessed tile on the board. 71 to 66, with two of their levies two
years out.

Rick's reading of it, which is the entry: the main mechanic is not
gaining points with agriculture, because you can't. It is interdiction,
and in that it is very effective. Agriculture destroys your own divine
realm and lets you destroy theirs faster.

Nothing changed in the engine. This is a name for something that has
been running since 1.1, and tick.js already carried the sentence above
the loop that does it — "the one-way ratchet: farmland does not
out-score blessing, it erases it."

Four lines make it true:

  a furrow scores 2 and blessing scores 3
  a furrow wipes every blessed tile touching it, to nobody
  you cannot bless farmland, ever — blessEffect returns null on reck
  ploughing is free, automatic, 3 tiles a year, 30 for a settlement's
    life; blessing costs an act and a walk, one tile at a time

Measured, 200 paired seeds against Cities, each doctrine compared with
itself on the same seeds against Passive:

                 builds alone   loses   takes   ends at
  cities              153        -74     -69      79
  mixed               165        -86     -58      79
  haunt               253       -151     -55     102
  bands               252       -157     -44      95

Against the same opponent, agriculture takes half again as much and
loses half as much. It simply cannot build. Bands makes 252 and keeps
95 — it loses 62% of everything it ever holds.

Blessing erased by the ratchet, 120 games: 8 to 15 tiles a game in a
magical-against-settled game, 35 a side when both powers plough.

called it noise; that is correct and beside the point, because Cities

Two things it opens, neither acted on: the score does not show a settled
player how they are doing — the number that matters to that doctrine is
the opponent's blessing going out, and the interface never names it. And
