Game 8 into A-33: the herds worked, and the cap is the live question

The first game played on the roaming build, and the direct refutation of game 6.
Same player, same shape of board. Game 6: both herds grazing on his own quiet
ground while the rival held seven ploughed towns — the weapon used as fertiliser.
Game 8: four bands, three of them grazing in the adversary's country at year
forty, the fourth walking. Rick: the herds did some actual work, and made the game
interesting. That is A-33's 18.3 tiles and -2.5 of their farmland seen once
instead of averaged over 322 games.

And it turns the cap from a free parameter into the live question. `bands` is 4
and he finished with exactly 4. Two stood at 128 and 140 against a fuse of 110 —
both over the line, neither able to split, both simply growing. The two at 64 are
the halves, so the fuse had fired at least twice earlier. The harness called this
2.2 capped crossings a game and filed it; here it is the end-state of a game
somebody played. Either the cap rises, or a band that cannot split has to do
something other than swell.

He raised no stone all game, which made kurgans impossible rather than rare and
left stonesGrow and deadOrders inert. Beside game 7, where two dead stones were
the only reach he had left, the pair say it cleanly: mounds, courses and relays
all sit downstream of a decision to raise a stone, and a settled player may never
make it. OP-12's suspicion is now a question about the stone, not the mound.

The game also ended at zero manifestation — no move, no tile to walk into, every
wonder struck through. Games 2 and 7 ended at a tenth; nobody had reached the
actual bottom of the stock before. And it kills the play-style note this project
has carried since game 4: 90 / 100 / 80 per cent, then 10, 10 and 0. Rick spends
himself. Three of the last four have gone to the bottom.
