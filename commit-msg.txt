Ideas: the autonomous levy, and what the engine says about an ending

Three entries in the dump, none of them committed to.

The shape of a turn, as a constraint. One movement, one act, one intervention,
forty times, and nothing new may add a fourth thing you do in a year. If a rule
needs management it either replaces one of the three or it runs itself. That is
why the herds had to be autonomous, in Rick's words.

A levy that raises itself and then goes — the generalisation of 1.24 rather than
a second special case. Written down with the objection attached: game 2's best
emergent result is a war fought by the people and paid for by the gods, and it
exists precisely because dreamWorks charges for orders issued out of hearing. An
autonomous levy deletes that cost and makes war free to a god. Worth deciding on
purpose.

And the ending as an assessment — will you be remembered — extrapolated from the
board rather than counted off it. Measured before arguing about it, because the
engine can already run the extrapolation: take both gods off the board and call
worldTick for two hundred more generations.

  storm v cities   your blessing  their blessing  farmland  bands
    gen  80             28.8          14.6          6.4      1.1
    gen 240             29.1          14.6          5.4      1.1

The world freezes the moment the gods stop, and it freezes at once. The board at
generation 240 is the board at generation 41 to a tile. Ploughing is a lifetime
budget of 30 per settlement and by year forty it is spent; blessing is only made
by a god or an audible settlement and by year forty almost nothing is audible.
Growth still runs and bigger buys nothing.

So "agriculture takes over by weight of numbers" is not a property of this engine,
and the payoff question turns out to be the population question that has been
sitting in the backlog as a note to self. If a settlement ploughed because it had
people to feed, the board would keep moving after the gods left and the
extrapolation would just be the engine run with nobody playing — no new weights, no
scoring lever, which is what OP-25's ninth rule wants.

One thing the frozen run did produce: the bands are the only thing still moving at
generation 240. Every settlement static, every furrow static, and the herds still
walking. They have no terminal state. Herds keep their mythology is not a thing
that needs building.

And a reason for kurgans at last. On a ploughed board a mound and a buried stone
are the only things that survive the erasure. If the ending asks what is left of
you in a thousand years, the answer for a god whose country went under the plough
is the graves and nothing else. OP-12's decoration, given a job by OP-25.
