A-19: the arc is a theme, and holding it off is a strategy

A played game, year 40, and the register learns more from it than from the last
three thousand simulated ones.

A hybrid line: a magical enclave walled off behind mountains and water, and
agricultural settlements founded in the middle of the board to compete with a
rival who did not wall anything and sprawled instead. It ended 73 to 83, a narrow
loss after leading for part of it, with both levies on the road and a year from
arriving.

The board said something the README does not allow for. Three settlements taught
to till, three working stones, and therefore zero wonders lost — lostCount
cancelled exactly, and all six were still in the row at year forty. "You end the
game holding everything you can build, and nothing you can call", and this player
ended holding both.

Ruled: that is a valid strategy and the player decides. Recorded as A-19, in
Rick's terms — the commitment was profound, and from a god's point of view it
makes sense to preserve a chosen people in a mystical place somewhere.

The part worth noticing is that concept.md already said so. Under What it is not:
"Not a tragedy on rails. The fading is caused by choices, including yours, and a
well-played game can hold a great deal of it off." This is the first time that
line has been exercised by an actual game rather than asserted in a design
document, so it now carries the example. The one sentence is the axis every
decision sits on; it was never a prophecy about where you have to end up.

Not a loophole, and A-19 says why. Three working stones means three connected
blessed regions held for forty years against a rival who can bless the ground out
from under them, and OP-16 measured that 92% of stones end under farmland. Walling
the enclave spends Raise mountains and Drown on terrain rather than on the rival,
which are the two most expensive things a player owns. Every tile inside the wall
is a tile not competing for the middle. The wonders were kept by spending the
whole early game on the conditions for keeping them, and the game was lost anyway.

It also gives the lore a third stance, arrived at from play rather than from
history. lore.md frames refusers against convinced and records Storm & Sky as
indifferent because nobody had a third answer for them. This is the preserver:
not no settling and not settle everything, but keep a few who can still hear you,
somewhere defensible, and let the rest count the fields.

OP-14's felt half is answered. The player finished at 10% of himself — one
movement point, four tiles reachable, still playing through his stones. Nobody had
ever been near the bottom of that stock; the trespass toll alone does not get you
there. It reads as a decision and not as a punishment, which is what needed a
person: "it felt like real decisions and considerations had to be made."

And it got there by a route nobody designed. Both powers spent themselves down
keeping levies moving, because dreamWorks charges for an order issued out of
hearing and in a close endgame both sides kept paying. What came out is a war
fought by the people and paid for by the gods. The harness could not have found
it — a one-ply chooser pays that toll without knowing it is paying, so the matrix
records the drain and cannot record what it is like to choose it. What stays open
is only the mechanical half: this game ended at 10%, not 0%, so nobody has stood
at the bottom yet.

OP-06 has a second played line. The first was a pure refuser at 108-75 with no
farmland at all. This is a hybrid at 73-83. Two people-played shapes, neither
winning by fiat, with the result in doubt to the last year — which is what that
entry has been asking for since it was raised. A third line is reported working
and unmeasured: walling the rival in with mountains and water, which makes those
two wonders strategic terrain-shaping rather than harassment, and is not how
either was priced.

OP-19 carries both readings of the stone brake side by side now, deliberately.
For a refuser it subtracts from zero and holds back nothing, which is still a
defect. For a hybrid it cancels teaching one-for-one, which is A-19 and stays.
Fixing the first must not re-break the second, and that is why they are in one
entry rather than two.

Instrumentation this wants and nobody has built: the wonder count as its own
series over a game, which OP-20 already asks for. Holding the arc off should be
an achievement. If the row is still full at year forty across several played
games, the brake is too generous.
