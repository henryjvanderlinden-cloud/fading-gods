1.7 charges a tenth of your manifestation for ending a year on reckoned
ground. It has never asked who ploughed it — the constant's comment says
"their fields" and the chronicle says "their furrows", and both were
wrong about the code. Rick's ruling keeps the code and corrects the
comments: gods have no place on tilled lands, it destroys them.

So the rule is untouched. What changed is the chooser, which had no
concept of a body at all. free() guarded the dream toll; nothing guarded
the standing one.

Three guards in engine/ai.js, each found by measuring the one before it,
and none of them an improvement in judgement:

  furrow / off      the best act that does not leave the token in a
                    furrow, and only if every candidate does, the best
                    act there is. The nothing-to-do fallback picks clean
                    ground too.
  notUnderfoot      a Clearance ploughs the six tiles round the one it is
                    aimed at, and 49 of the 51 tolls that survived the
                    first guard were a god putting the plough through its
                    own feet.
  stepOff           the act and the order happen where you stand and then
                    you walk on. reach() again, with the movement the act
                    did not spend, to the nearest clean tile. A god that
                    used the whole year getting there stays in what it
                    ordered.
