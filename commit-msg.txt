Three rules from the interesting pile, and a fourth that had never once run

Rick's pick: encirclement and the taboo, the Bless/Quicken split, and split at
path distance 2 — "and check the split logic thoroughly, because I haven't been
able to use it."

He hasn't been able to use it because it has never been legal. Not tuned badly,
not rarely available: impossible, in every position, since it was written.
`canSplit` asked for a neighbour of the settlement that passed the whole founding
test, and `foundBlock` refuses any tile with a settlement in its own
neighbourhood — which every neighbour of a settlement has, namely the settlement
doing the splitting. The two conditions are mutually exclusive by construction.
Measured before anything was touched: **true in 0 of 1,047 settlement-years**,
playing the doctrine that weights it highest. A-20.

`bands` weights split at 14, second only to raising a stone. Its whole share of
the chooser's attention has been going to an option that could not be selected,
so every number ever measured for the doctrine the dropdown calls *splits at
sixty* is a number for a doctrine playing with one fewer act than it thinks it
has. Fifth thing in this project found by reading the code against the prose
rather than by measuring, and the register's rule that produced it holds again: a
legality test that is always false produces no anomaly, only an absence, and the
harness cannot report an absence.

## 1.6 — where a splinter goes

Ground you have blessed, at path distance 2, with the 85% founding requirement
waived. Everything else `foundBlock` asks is still asked, and one of those
questions does the shaping: *not beside a settlement* rules out distance 1 by
itself, so two is not a chosen radius, it is the only one available. Path
distance rather than ring distance (A-18) — a place ringed by water has nowhere
to send anyone, and a split cannot leapfrog terrain.

The blessing requirement is kept and the percentage dropped: the people are
already there, and what they need is somewhere quiet to stop, not a country.
That keeps §3's *all expansion begins with blessing* true of splitting as well as
founding.

One price nobody had written, because nothing had ever done it: a settlement
stands on wild ground, so **the founding spends that tile's blessing.** Splitting
costs you a tile of your own quiet country, permanently.

In the build it now arms and waits for the tile, like an intervention. Where the
splinter lands is a founding, and founding is the one thing every expansion in
this game begins with; it should not be a random pick out of a list of one. A-21.

## 1.8 — taking their ground returns it to wild

OP-16's Bless/Quicken split, built on **presence** rather than on which spell it
is. Bless happens under your feet and always unmakes. Quicken asks whether its
target was within arm's reach — the same test `tolled` uses, so the game's two
prices are read off one line — and sent down the stone network it takes wild
ground and passes over theirs.

*Creation at a distance, unmaking only in person*, said on the board instead of
asserted in a register. It answers OP-16's original complaint without cutting
Quicken's reach, which that entry had already warned would be a net nerf to the
magical player.

**The spread it produced is not about blessing. It is about turn order.** A-17's
swing exists because Bless overwrote, so the second mover overwrote after the
first had committed. Take the overwrite away and most of the mechanism goes with
it. Mirror matches, 60 games, mean margin with the starting-position bias
subtracted:

| doctrine | order was worth | with 1.8 | with all three |
|---|---|---|---|
| cities | −1.1 | −8.3 | **−19.4** |
| haunt | −11.6 | **−6.5** | −6.5 |
| bands | −14.5 | **−1.8** | +1.2 |

So this is A-17's second candidate arriving by another road. It is not
`BLESS_WILD_ONLY` — it takes their ground and gives it to nobody rather than
refusing to take it — but it removes the same overwrite. A-17 is not reopened; it
is re-pointed, and OP-21's fourth candidate, letting the second player choose
their seat, is worth more now than when it was written. A-22.

## 1.9 — the ring closes, and the place is forbidden

Two years ringed and the settlement changes hands. Rock and water count as part
of the ring, so a coastal place is closed in by three tiles rather than six —
accepted deliberately, and now built.

**The taboo is not offered, it is what happens.** Rick's call, and it deletes the
entry's hardest problem instead of answering it: a captured ringed-in place is
forbidden both teachings, for good. OP-20's argument that forbidding must return
a wonder or nobody would choose it was written when it *was* a choice. It is not
one now, and the wonder moves anyway — `lostCount` is derived from the board, so
un-teaching the place drops the count of whoever owned it and the wonder goes
back to the side that lost the loud city. Exactly what taking a taught city by
levy already does. No stored counter. The note above `lostCount` predicted this
in the same commit that moved the trigger onto teaching, and it was right.

Which makes the trade sharper than the entry imagined: you stop their farmland
engine, take three points of settlement, and **hand them back a wonder.** Your
rival comes out weaker agriculturally and more dangerous magically, and nobody
designed that.

Everything downstream needed no new rule. `carryCap` reads `taught`, so the
ceiling falls to Dunbar the moment the place is forbidden and the decline floors
itself at 28% a year: 929 → 669 → 481 → 347 → 250 → 197 → 177 → 167 → 161. The
ones who leave become refugee columns on §7's machinery, and they walk back to
the country of the power that **lost** them, because they are leaving over the
plough and they go where ploughing is still allowed. Forbidding one loud place
makes the rival's next town bigger. You cannot forbid your way to silence.

## The rule that had to be added, and it is the good one

The mechanic did not fire. Farmland erases blessing; a taught settlement ploughs
its own first ring; a ring is made of blessing; and blessing cannot be laid on
farmland. So one season's ploughing broke every siege **permanently**, and a
taught settlement — precisely the one OP-20 exists to be able to take — was
structurally immune. Before the fix, a 900-strong city under a closed ring grew
929 → 950 → 965 for nine years.

**A place closed in on every side does not go out to the fields.** One line, and
it is what a siege is. It also hands the defender the counter the entry had no
answer for: break the ring — a tile of your own blessing beside the place, a
Wither, or a Clearance ordered onto a besieging tile from another of your
settlements — and the fields resume the same year and the clock goes back to
zero. **Relief by clearance is a settled-doctrine answer to a magical attack**,
which this board did not have, and it is not written anywhere. It falls out.

A mature city still cannot be ringed until its existing farmland has been
Withered away, so taking one is a three-stage project over several years. That is
the late-game work OP-20 asked for, and no one-ply chooser will ever attempt it.
A-23.

## Measured

80 games a cell against Cities, the build's own turn order. The first row
reproduces the table in OP-19 exactly, so these are comparable.

| | cities | mixed | haunt | bands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the batch as it was | 50% | 34% | 55% | 34% |
| + 1.6 split alone | 45% | **43%** | 48% | **39%** |
| + 1.8 unmake alone | 40% | 30% | **66%** | **24%** |
| + 1.9 encircle alone | 56% | 40% | **70%** | 44% |
| **all three, shipped** | 34% | 36% | 54% | 29% |

1.6 gives an act back to the doctrines that weight it and takes the points off
the two that do not, which is what a repair should look like. 1.8 and 1.9 pull
hard in opposite directions on the blessing doctrines and largely cancel. Haunt
lands at 54% against 55% before, which is not a result anybody aimed at.

**Left untuned**, on the standing rule: tuning an unfinished rule set means
tuning it twice, and none of OP-19's knobs has moved yet either.

**And the matrix undercounts 1.9 and always will.** A one-ply chooser never
builds a ring on purpose — every capture measured is an accident of blessing, 0.2
to 0.5 a game. The number that matters will come from a played game.
