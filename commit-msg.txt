SOL's reading, and OP-27 / OP-28 / OP-29

Written in another session and left uncommitted in the working tree; committed
here unchanged. Not my text and not adjudicated — the entry says so itself.

OpenAI's gpt-5.6-sol, running as Codex on this machine, was given read-only access
to the repository and asked what the project is, what is good in it and what it
would change. It read README.md and design/ and skimmed engine/. It has not played
a game, has not run the harness, and never saw a number.

Three items it raises that the register did not already carry:

  OP-27 · high    · is the first-game ruleset too large to be played? A
                    complexity budget: ship a smaller ruleset, make each mechanic
                    earn its way back in. Settled at the board, in OP-21, by
                    asking the player what a move will cost before they make it
                    and recording the gap. The A/B already exists — R2all(false)
                    plays the smaller game.
  OP-28 · medium  · are irreversible consequences visible before they are chosen?
                    A forecast in the terms panel, watching for the opposite
                    failure — perfect foresight turns temptation back into
                    arithmetic.
  OP-29 · medium  · nothing records a game well enough to re-run it. No action
                    log, no deterministic save/load, though the state is already
                    serialisable. The only item in the review with no existing
                    home here.

Its first recommendation is OP-21 — two people at one board — reached
independently and from documents alone, without knowing it was already ranked
highest. That convergence is the most useful thing in the entry.

It also caught a real error the register did not have: README.md line 19 says
"no build step, no dependencies, no server" while package.json declares jsdom
^30.0.1 under `dependencies`. jsdom is a tool for the headless checks and not for
the game; it belongs under devDependencies. Unfixed.
