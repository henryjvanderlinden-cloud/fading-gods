OP-21: two people at one board, built

A second person is an option in the other seat, and choosing it restarts,
because it changes the shape of a year rather than the strength of an
opponent. The year goes left seat, hand over, right seat, and only then does
the world move.

The engine's one structural assumption about who is human is gone. endYear()
no longer hardcodes aiTurn(1), and the year reset clears both seats rather
than seat 0 alone. Neither can move a measured number - ai.js reads neither
acted nor cast - and node sim/matrix.js 40 40 cities reproduces §10 exactly
at 57/8/43/13, which is how that claim is checked rather than asserted.
ui.js addresses one SEAT variable instead of 52 literals, and the map click
handler reads it at click time rather than closing over it, so the cached
land layer survives a hand-over.

Hotseat is cheaper here than a hotseat usually is, and the reason is worth
recording: there is no hidden information in this game, so no curtain is
needed between turns. What is needed instead is knowing whose year it is
from across a table, so the active seat is carried by colour across the full
width of the board rather than by a label in a side panel.

The hand-over stays a toggle, off by default, with the condition stated as
nothing further is possible rather than everything is used.

The compensating tile is in, off, and not validated. It went in on a clean
argument - a blessed tile is worth 3, OP-07's left-seat bias is +4.0, so one
tile very nearly cancels it - and sim/handicap.js does not agree. Haunt moves
nearly 8, because a blessed tile also enlarges the connected region a stone's
power is measured over and gives a tile to bless outward from. Bands moves
the wrong way. At 160 games a cell that looked like +10.8 and alarming; at
400 it is +3.0 ± 3.8 and is noise. Nothing is resolved at these sample sizes
- OP-07's own figure took 1,160 games - and the likely mechanism is OP-01
again, since a pre-blessed start tile lowers blessGain under the token and a
one-ply chooser then takes a different first act. The numbers are in OP-07
rather than the toggle being quietly kept.

If that doctrine-dependence turns out to be real it has exactly the disease
A-17's bias has: a bias whose sign depends on how you play cannot be
corrected by a static handout to one seat. The answer for both is the same
one, and it is a fourth candidate the register had never considered - let the
second player choose their seat. Cities wants to act first and blessing
doctrines want to act second, so seat choice converts the asymmetry into a
decision rather than a handicap. That is A-10's move, and it costs no engine
change.

The chronicle is not done, and deliberately. One log, folded shut and
labelled told from the left hand, which is honest and costs nothing. Two
properly addressed logs is a mechanical change to about forty say() call
sites and wants its own pass.

Board size and mis-tapping are recorded in OP-21 and deferred. smoke.js now
plays a two-seat game through the build's own buttons, 11,758 checks, and
asserts the thing that matters: that handing over does not move the world.
