OP-24: the herds leave your hand

1.24 `roam`, built behind its own flag, on. The three handles come off —
steering, stopping, and raising a mound — and `roamTick` plays herds for both
seats in the tick. Rick's ruling of 26/29 August, harder than the entry proposed
at every point where there was a softer reading.

A year of theirs, and the order is the argument. They re-aim every year, because
they are opportunists and nothing holds them to last year's road. They walk at
the nearest ploughed ground of the adversary or a silent stone of their own god,
ranked together — a band crossing a valley to eat a furrow buries a shrine on the
way without being told to, and OP-16 says 92% of stones end under exactly that
ground. Failing both, the open ground beside a town of theirs. They graze the one
tile they arrive on, never scaled by how many of them there are: splitting has to
be the escalation and not a relief to the man being eaten. They raise the mound
free and automatic, no act, no fifth, no year, and no field needed under it —
*they build their monument in their spare time with the materials at hand*. Then
the grass has its say, and only then are the absorbed added.

That last ordering is the ceiling question OP-24 could not answer. The logistic
pulls a band back toward the Seventy-Seven; absorption is the only thing that
pushes it past. So a band eating fields stays a band and a band gnawing at a city
swells — big towns breed what eats them.

`absCap` was measured before it was believed. Without it a band beside a city of
a thousand takes sixty a year, blows the fuse in two, splits to `bands` and then
balloons: 4.1 fuse-crossings a game of which 3.7 were already capped, and a band
of 302 on the board. Fifteen is off the curve, not off feel — the logistic sheds
n·0.32·(n/77−1), so +12 levels at 105, +15 at 110, +20 at 118, which is OP-24's
table arrived at from the other end.

And they end one way. Boxed in — nothing of theirs in reach and no road to any of
it — they put the roofs back up **taught to till**. 1.19 called stopping a detour
and that is now false: herding is a door, and it opens onto the plough. It is
still yours, a wonder goes for it through `lostCount`, and nothing was gained.
The two paragraphs in `constants.js` that promised otherwise are corrected in
place rather than deleted, because the promises were real.

Checks: 11,973, all passing. Both old fingerprints exact — and *that is the
finding*, because `bands` and `cities` never teach herding, so neither array
could see this rule. A third fingerprint on `storm` v `cities` goes in beside
them, with the steered game frozen underneath it.

Measured, 200 paired seeds, 79 of them games where a band ever walked:

                     win     score      their farmland   their big towns
  steered           50.6%   79.9:78.3        13.7              5.0
  roaming           44.3%   74.2:74.2        13.3              5.2

Null, or a little worse for the side that uses it, and well inside the interval
at 79 games. Per game the rule does run: 1.4 bands, 13.5 band-years, 6.3 tiles
grazed, 112 people taken, 0.3 splits, 0.6 settlings, 0.4 mounds. What it does not
do is show up in the score, and the likeliest reason is written down rather than
tuned away — a band that settles comes back taught and costs a wonder, where
stopping one used to be three free points. Gameplay first, scoring after: OP-25.

Kurgans still almost never happen, and now for a different reason. The trigger
works; `storm` builds 0.73 stones a game and 0.27 of them go silent, so there is
nothing to trigger it on. That is a chooser artifact, not a rule.

Two things left unruled and left alone rather than decided quietly: grazing is
still owner-blind, so a band crossing its own side's fields eats them; and the
cap of four binds in the games that matter.
