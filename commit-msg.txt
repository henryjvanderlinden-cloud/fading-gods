lore.md: the thread underneath, and the balance verdict overturned

Rick's own framing of the thesis, written down while it is fresh, because it only
existed in a conversation.

The loss of innocence is the first move and not the ending — Jaynes's bicameral
mind, Adam and Eve, a common thread through human history. And the rule for it has
been in the build since 1.5 with nobody calling it that: `audible` is *a people few
enough and untaught enough that there is nothing between them and you but air*.
Under seventy-seven they bless the ground by being there; past it they cannot hear
you; being shown the plough ends it too, because a people shown the plough are
counting the fields, not listening. That is Jaynes as a population threshold and
Eden as a teaching, in one predicate, and nobody set out to write it — it fell out
of asking who can hear a god.

The pastoralists are already historically shaped: herding may only be taught once
somebody has broken ground, which is a condition rather than a clock and happens
to be the real dependency. Since 1.24 they walk out of hearing and never come back,
and in a world where the gods have stopped they are the only thing still moving.

The three layers — hunter-gatherers, Anatolian farmers, Yamnaya — are all in the
game, with the right dependency structure. What is missing is mixture, and mixture
is the point of the comparison: the layers absorbed each other, and what came out
carried fragments of all three. Here ownership is binary and encirclement is a
clean erasure, which is the one thing the real story never does.

So "only some myths survive" is a scoring principle, not a mood. The myths that
lasted did so inside somebody else's people. The question an ending should ask is
not what do you still hold but what is still being carried by people who are no
longer yours — the only version under which losing the ground and being remembered
anyway makes sense. And that is where the kurgans have been pointing since 1.19: a
mound is memory rather than possession, and a tile count has no way to value a
grave.
