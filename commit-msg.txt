Four rules, and every flag on

The batch Rick asked for after reading the open points register: stones that
grow, dead stones that carry orders, the wild folk as a founding population, and
an answer to what zero manifestation is. All four ship on, and so does everything
else that is built — `herds` and `barren3` come off the bench with them.

**`built` and `on` are the same set again**, which they have not been since 1.12
and 1.19 shipped switched off. Eighteen built rules, eighteen on. `pathFrac` is
the only flag that is false and that is because nothing reads it.

## 1.20 · stonesGrow · a stone grows while it is heard

Closes *stones grow* in `ideas.md` as **A-27**. A stone gains a course, one a
year and three at most, while an untaught settlement under seventy-seven or a
herd stands in its reach. The entry's own design question was never the bonus but
*who does the adding*, because a stone that grows on an age counter is a timer
and A-10 would be raised against it correctly.

What a course buys is the **working threshold**: six connected blessed tiles down
to three. An old stone remembers a larger country than it now stands in, so
severing a blessed region hurts an ancient stone less than a new one.

Three things fall out and all three are why it is this shape. Teach that band to
till and the stone stops — not by a rule but because a taught people are counting
the fields, and it stands arrested and visibly unfinished for the rest of the
game. Farmland still wins: a stone under the plough has no blessed region at all
and three courses do not save it, so OP-16's 92% is untouched. And it may not
feed the wonder brake, so `lostCount` reads `workingStrict` — the plain six —
while every other caller reads the augmented test, with the reason written
between the two functions.

A herd counts as audible and builds the stone, which the entry did not anticipate
and which is the answer to *a herd with nothing left to graze has no behaviour*.

## 1.21 · deadOrders · a stone that has gone quiet still carries orders

Closes **OP-13** as **A-28**, at Rick's range of two. Working stones carry
presence, dead stones carry orders: a work aimed within two tiles of a silent
stone of yours arrives free of 1.17's toll.

It changes `tolled` and nothing else. The works keep the reach they always had —
`targets()` still builds them from the settlement outward. What is extended is
the country an *order* arrives in. Creation still travels through living stones
alone: a dead stone relays no wonder and teaches nobody.

A kurgan is excluded. *Buried stones carry memory* is OP-13's own third line, so
raising a mound closes the relay for good — which gives the mound a cost, and
therefore a decision, which is the thing OP-15 has never been able to give it.

## 1.22 · wildFolk · a founding is as big as the country round it

Closes **OP-18** as **A-29**. Twenty to forty people, by how much of the eighteen
tiles two rings out is blessed ground of yours. Rock, water and the edge of the
map count in the denominator and never in the numerator, so a coastal founding
starts smaller than one in the middle of a country.

Found only. No pool, nothing to spend, read once and never again — which is what
keeps `concept/concept.md`'s *no economy to manage* true, and which is the
objection that chose this version over the register's.

## 1.23 · zeroSpent · spent to nothing, you may only watch

Closes the last open half of **OP-14** as **A-30**. There is no floor. At nothing
left you cannot move, act, teach, order or intervene. The year still turns, the
score still accrues, the stones still bless, your people still plough and march
and graze — and you watch it. The game does not end; your part in it does.

The register's own alternative was *lose the body and keep playing as a network*,
and Rick's is the harder version: a network with no location is still a player
taking turns, and this is not. It is the title said out loud.

Both powers thin toward transparency as their manifestation falls, wherever
`fade` is on. At a tenth you are a phantom standing in a field, and so is the
other one. That drawing is half the rule.

## One change to the chooser, recorded rather than slipped in

`free()` in `ai.js` now declines a tolled target near the bottom of the stock
instead of falling back to it, keeping two tolls in reserve. Before it, seat 1
spent itself to nothing in 27 games out of 100 and stood paralysed for the rest
of them, moving every number below for a reason unrelated to any rule here. It is
not an improvement in judgement — it does not weigh the last tenth of a body
against the work it would buy, which is precisely the decision 1.23 exists to
hand a player. OP-01, and it is the second time this year the chooser has been
the largest term in a measurement.

**Bands: 10% in the pre-batch game, 28% after the August batch, 41% now.** OP-06
asked for *a magical victory should be possible, and hard*. That is the sentence.

Two rules do the work and neither is the one that was expected to. **`wildFolk`
is worth eleven points to Bands** and was proposed as mainly cosmetic; it is
A-09's *make blessing worth more* arriving thematically rather than as a tuned
constant. **`deadOrders` is worth nine to Mixed** and costs Haunt four — a
settled-side rule landing on the settled side, which is the first time anything
in this project has managed that on purpose.

`stonesGrow` and `zeroSpent` measure at nothing, and both nulls have one cause:
each is about a decision a one-ply chooser cannot make. `taughtLoss` and
`audible77` measured at exactly zero for the same reason and shipped anyway.
OP-01 is why. OP-21 is the instrument.

225 foundings: min 24, median 31, max 40, mean 31.1 — the mean is what the flat
constant always was, so nothing has been handed out, and *where* now decides how
it starts. 242 stones, mean 1.35 courses, three reached. A power spends itself to
nothing in 17 of 60 games, mean year 33, never before year 24.

`sim/smoke.js` at **11,924 checks**, all passing, with a constructed-board block
for all four rules and both fingerprints re-frozen. The pre-batch baseline did
not move, which is the check that says nothing leaked. `sim/sweep.js` is new and
produces the table above.

## What has not been done

Nobody has played any of this. The four questions that need a person: does the
last tenth of yourself feel like something you spend or something you lose; is a
network of dead stones a decision or a chore; does a stone with three courses on
it read as a thing the people built; and does being handed a relay by an attacker
who took your holy place feel like the joke it looks like on paper.
