The works open on what you taught them, and they open at all

OP-05 was never a mid-game sag. Read from the code and then measured: the works
had stopped opening. Under the batch, with the old ladder, any work opened in 28%
of Cities games and 13% of Mixed, first one around year 33, never at all for
either refusing doctrine, and the levy fired once in forty games. The mean Cities
game ended at strength 3.1 against a first gate of 5 — below the bottom rung of a
three-rung ladder. Section 6's "you begin with none and gain them" had quietly
stopped being true, and the chip row OP-23 built to empty from the left and fill
from the right only ever did the first of those.

The cause is logistic growth moving both distributions out from under a ladder
priced for the old 2600 clamp. An untaught settlement is asymptotic to kWild=150
and never counts at all, so a refuser sat at strength 0 for forty years. A taught
one now tops out between 600 and 1000, so the 800 rung is nearly unreachable.
Same shape as the stone brake subtracting from zero: correct under the old growth
model, quietly not under the new one, and nothing breaks loudly.

taughtGates. The ladder counts settlements taught to till — 1 opens clearance,
2 the colony, 3 the levy. Chosen over simply lowering 5/7/9, which measures the
same, for two reasons. lostCount already reads that number, so one counter now
runs the arc in both directions: every teaching costs you a wonder and buys you a
work, and the game was computing that twice, differently, and showing the player
only half of it. And taughtCount is already on the stat bar, so the gate is a
number being looked at — which is what OP-05 has wanted since it was raised, for
two lines.

landGates is cut without ever being built, and moved to rejected.md with the
reasoning. Reading the unlock off tilled ground measured no better and cost a
per-settlement unlock model, which would also have changed what a dim chip in the
row means — from "you have not come far enough" to "not from this place". That is
a real change to the interface's grammar for no measured gain. The entry says
plainly that it was never built, because a cut proposal left in a live register is
how it comes back looking attractive.

The balance result is the thing to take away. 80 games a cell, seeds split:

                              cities  mixed  haunt  bands
  the old game                55%     38%    11%    10%
  the batch, old 5/7/9        53%     46%    79%    63%
  the batch, taught 1/2/3     50%     34%    55%    34%

Haunt 79 to 55 and Bands 63 to 34, from giving the settled side its late game
back. Larger than any knob OP-19 lists — r, the taught caps, the reckoning budget
— and it is not a nerf to the magical line, because nothing about blessing
changed. So part of what the register has been reading as "the batch made magic
strong" was really "the batch switched the settled endgame off". The overshoot is
smaller than it looked and partly diagnosed rather than merely tuned. None of the
stated knobs has been touched.

Works now open in 85% of Cities games and 91% of Mixed, first one about year 23,
all three in roughly two thirds of Cities games.

The pre-batch path keeps its own frozen 5/7/9 rather than borrowing the sliders,
which now read in teachings. FG.R2all(false) still plays exactly section 6. A
baseline that moves with a slider is not a baseline — A-16, in miniature.

The locked-chip reason is read from FG.civicNeed() rather than assumed by the
interface, and smoke.js asserts the wording, so the rule and the row cannot start
naming different things.

Left alone on purpose, one thing at a time: the per-settlement minimums in
targets() at 150/200/300, and A-13's costs in people at 10/35/45, which were
priced against a ladder that has now been replaced and against works that in
practice never opened. Both are recorded as live in OP-05, along with a feedback
loop nobody has measured — teachings per game rose from 3.8 to 5.0 when the gates
came down, because a colony is born tilling.
