The third leg, built and left switched off

`FG.R2.herds`, 1.19. OP-12 has wanted a third leg since before the current
design and never had one. It has one now: a people never shown the plough may
be taught to keep herds instead, and then they stop standing still.

    farmland beats blessing   the ratchet — furrows erase the quiet beside them
    herds beat farmland       they overrun it and graze it back to thorn
    blessing beats herds      a rival power's blessing is closed country to them

**It ships off.** That is as much the point of the commit as the rule is. This is
the largest engine change since armies, going in on top of a batch that has been
played exactly once, and this project's own evidence hierarchy puts a person above
the matrix for anything about choices. So it is built, tested, drawn — and dark
until somebody sits in front of it. The batch of 1.6 / 1.8 / 1.9 is still exactly
the thing that was measured and played.

Three decisions, all taken before a line was written, all Rick's, all archived:

- **A-24 — no wonder, and no points.** `lostCount` counts settlements taught to
  till and a herd is not a settlement, so nothing is taken from you: they were
  never shown the plough, they still hear you, nothing about you has gone quieter.
  And nothing is given either — a herd holds no ground and `score()` never sees
  it. They deny; they do not accumulate. Neither half works alone. Let them score
  and the central trade can simply be declined; charge a wonder and herding is a
  strictly worse plough that nobody ever takes.
- **A-25 — grazed ground goes to wild, and stays barren three years.** Not to you:
  the herd unmakes and the god still has to walk out and make. And with A-14's
  lifetime budget already in place, grazed ground is lost twice — once when the
  furrows go, and again when re-ploughing spends a tile of a settlement's thirty
  that it had already spent. The second cost is invisible and permanent.
- **A-26 — the other power's blessing is closed country.** They move the way their
  god does rather than the way an army does, because a people who were never
  taught the plough can still hear, and what a people can hear they can be shut
  out of. *Theirs* only — blessing impassable to herds would wall your own herds
  in with your own quiet, which is the self-walling problem OP-19 already strained
  to breaking.

What it costs to send a people out, and none of it had to be written: the three
points the place was worth, everything it would have grown into, and most of the
people — `kHerd` is the Seventy-Seven, and a village that has been standing a
while is above it and sheds the surplus. Going nomadic costs you most of a town.

**Herding is not a door out of the central dilemma, it is a detour.** A herd that
stops comes back untaught. You may walk around the fork for twenty years and you
arrive at the same fork, with fewer people and nothing scored in between. That is
the answer to the one objection OP-12 has never been able to answer, and it is why
the rule survives at all.

**Kurgans came with it.** A herd on farmland over a stone of its own god that has
already stopped working may raise a mound: a fifth of them, and the year. The
ground stays reckoned — a mound in a field — and the stone counts as *standing*
without ever working, blessing, or feeding the wonder brake. Working stones carry
presence, dead stones carry orders, buried stones carry memory. OP-16 measured
that 92% of stones end under farmland and that it is irreversible; this does not
reverse it, it makes it mean something. A refuser cannot have their shrines back
as engines, and can have them back as graves.

`moundCount` gates nothing and scores nothing, deliberately. It is for OP-15's
*Forgotten*, which is unwritten — so that entry is now the blocker on half of a
built rule, and if the endings never arrive the mounds are decoration.

**1.12 `barren3` is half-built alongside, and stays off.** Barren ground exists in
the engine and grazing writes it; the flag now governs only whether *Wither* does
too. One writer per rule, one reader for both. It is a separate question and it
moves four numbers on its own — do not turn it on with 1.19.

**A played game found the bug, in the way things get found here.** Year 32 of seed
7: *a herd is standing on a settlement*. The greedy chooser had dropped a colony
onto a camp, because `foundBlock` had never heard of herds. Found, colony and
split all had to refuse a camp; so did Drown and Raise mountains; and
`mountainLine` needed its own guard, because that loop keeps a private copy of the
exclusions and only the aimed-at tile was ever checked. Fourth instance of the
standing lesson: the legality test and the thing that mutates the board are
written twice in this engine, and they drift.

**Built-and-off broke something the harness was leaning on.** `FG.R2built(true)`
used to mean both *everything built* and *the game*; it does not any more.
`FG.R2reset()` is the game, and `sim/smoke.js` now freezes a fingerprint of that
as well as of the pre-batch baseline — the old one only ever asserted that a new
rule could not reach `R2all(false)`, which is a claim about a game nobody plays.

Measured, 80 games a cell against Cities, the build's own turn order:

|                          | cities | mixed | haunt | bands | storm |
|--------------------------|--------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| the game as it ships     |   34%  |  36%  |  54%  |  29%  |  63%  |
| + herds                  |   34%  |  36%  |  54%  |  29%  |  61%  |
| + barren3 (Wither only)  |   39%  |  44%  |  50%  |  26%  |  66%  |

The first row reproduces §10 exactly. The second row is the finding: **the rule
moves nothing.** Four doctrines identical to the digit; storm wins 63% without it
and 61% with it. Over 300 games across every doctrine pairing, the chooser taught
herding 1.65 times a game, grazed 2.3 tiles, spent more than half of every
herd-year standing still, settled a herd once, and raised zero kurgans.

That is the expected result and not a disappointment. A one-ply chooser cannot
decide that a place is worth more walking than standing, will not drive a people
four years across the board to reach a city's fields, and never rings anything on
purpose. OP-19 recorded `taughtLoss` and `audible77` measuring at exactly zero for
the same reason. **OP-21 is the instrument for this, and it exists.**

The interface: a third teaching chip, only in the row when the rule is on, saying
*nobody has broken ground anywhere yet* rather than *none of yours is in reach*;
three action buttons that appear when they apply, because nine buttons wrap the
row onto a second line and the four labelled rows are the layout — board measured
at 1038px in every state, unchanged. Herds drawn as a horned token with a loosely
dotted line to where they are going; barren ground as a scatter of dashes; kurgans
as a low barrow with kerb stones, redrawn once after looking at it, because the
first version was a hump and a hump was already taken twice over.

`sim/smoke.js` at 11,884 checks, all passing, with a constructed-board section for
every rule in 1.19 and both frozen fingerprints.

Nobody has played it. That is the whole of what is open. OP-12.
