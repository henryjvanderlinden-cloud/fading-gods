OP-24: the herds should leave your hand

Rick's, and raised as the point of the game rather than as a balance
idea: setting things in motion that you can no longer personally
control. Four parts — they walk themselves at the nearest adversarial
furrows, they absorb people from a settlement they stand beside, they
split above some size, and the ground they lay waste stays waste about
three years. The fourth is already built; wither is 3, and A-14 means
grazed ground is lost twice.

Three arguments for it are written down. It is the design value as a
mechanic. It is the answer to A-32 that the magical side does not have —
a band that walks into their fields by itself is the mirror of the
plough, and the only proposal so far that gives blessing a passive
denial engine without giving it points. And it is the one rule in the
build that gets MORE measurable by becoming autonomous: A-24/25/26
measured herds at a flat null because a one-ply chooser cannot drive
them, and taking the driving away makes both seats play them correctly.

What it takes away is named rather than discovered. 1.19 promises that a
herd is always audible, in exchange for scoring nothing. Autonomy
withdraws exactly that, and the compensation has to be replaced or the
sentence struck on purpose. The sharper question is what handle the
player keeps at all: steering, stopping, and the kurgan. If all three
go, teaching herding becomes a one-way act, which is the version worth
building first.

Four collisions found by reading first:

  audibleHerd is n < 77, so ANY absorption silences the band and it
  stops feeding stone courses under 1.20. Two rules from one batch
  cancel on contact.

  the logistic eats the absorption. Starting at 77 and taking +5/+10/
  +20/+40 a year, a band settles at 90/101/118/144 and NEVER reaches
  two Seventy-Sevens. A split threshold of 154 can never fire.

  splitting has a precedent and it is not split2, which sends half a
  settlement to blessed ground a roving band has none of.

  movement is already written. driveHerds is exactly it. Part one is
  mostly deleting the player's steering, not writing pathfinding.
